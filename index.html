Research in the Field of Drones & Components

Medium Priority ▾

Unit 1.1 – Electronics Industry Overview

  • Electronics industry: design, manufacture, assembly & servicing of electronic products
  • Key segments: Semiconductors, Industrial Equipment, Consumer Electronics, Medical, Telecom

Unit 1.2 – Types of Drones

  • Fixed-Wing: Like airplanes, need forward motion to generate lift, long endurance
  • Multi-Rotor: Most common; Quadcopter (4), Hexacopter (6), Octocopter (8)
  • Single-Rotor: Like helicopters, complex, expensive, high maintenance
  • Fixed-Wing Hybrid VTOL: Combines fixed wing + rotors — best of both worlds

Unit 1.3 – Opportunities & Trends

  • Applications: Photography, delivery, search & rescue, agriculture, surveillance, mapping
  • Emerging: Autonomous drones, AI/ML integration, Urban Air Mobility (UAM)

Unit 1.4 – Role of Drone Specialist

  • BOQ (Bill of Quantities) preparation, component sourcing, R&D activities

Study of Block Diagram of Drones ⭐

HIGH Priority ▾

Unit 2.1 – Functional Block Diagram & Dynamics

  • Lift: Upward force against gravity; created by pressure difference across rotating blades
  • Thrust: Force for forward motion
  • Drag: Air resistance opposing motion
  • Weight: Downward gravitational force

Quadcopter Movements – MUST KNOW

  • Throttle/Hover: All 4 propellers at normal speed → descend; all at high speed → ascend (hover)
  • Pitch: Forward/backward movement about lateral axis. Rear props faster → forward; Front props faster → backward
  • Roll: Side-to-side movement about longitudinal axis
  • Yaw: Rotation about vertical axis (clockwise/counter-clockwise)
💡 Exam Tip: Diagonal motors spin in opposite directions to cancel torque. CW and CCW propellers are paired diagonally.

Kinematics Formulas

  • Thrust: F = k·ω² (proportional to square of angular velocity)
  • Mx = (F₁ - F₃) × L (Roll moment)
  • My = (F₂ - F₄) × L (Pitch moment)
  • Newton's 2nd Law: Force = mass × acceleration; Torque = Inertia × angular acceleration

Major Drone Components

  • Frame, Propellers, Motors, ESC, Battery, Flight Controller, GPS, Sensors
  • Distribution Board (Power Distribution), Landing Gear, Control Station (Tx/Rx)
3

Required Constituents / Tools to Assemble a Drone

Medium Priority ▾

Unit 3.1 – Components for Assembly

  • Frame: Must be sturdy with low aerodynamic resistance; holds all components
  • Propellers: Long props → more thrust, heavier loads, low RPM; Short props → less load, fast response, high RPM
  • Control Station: Radio transmitter + receiver, data management components, remote control
  • Landing Gear: Protects drone on landing, maintains ground clearance for payload
💡 Exam Tip: Know the BOM (Bill of Materials) concept — identifying all components needed before assembly.
4

Study of Drone Electronics / Circuits ⭐

HIGH Priority ▾

Unit 4.1 – Basic Electronic Components

  • 5 types: Resistors, Capacitors, Transistors, Coils/Inductors, Diodes
  • Active components: Transistors, ICs (require power)
  • Passive components: Resistors, Capacitors, Inductors (don't require power)

Motors – BLDC (Brushless DC)

  • Most drones use BLDC motors for efficiency, power, and longevity
  • Motor Naming (e.g. 2213-935KV): 22 = stator width (mm), 13 = stator height (mm), 935KV = RPM per volt
  • Lower KV = more torque → larger props; Higher KV = more RPM → smaller props, racing drones
  • Motor spin: Diagonal pairs spin same direction; adjacent motors spin opposite

ESC – Electronic Speed Controller

  • Controls motor speed by converting DC battery power to 3-phase AC for BLDC motors
  • Works like a Variable Frequency Drive (VFD)
  • Types: Single ESC (one per motor) or 4-in-1 ESC (all on one board)
  • Modern protocols: DShot150, DShot300, DShot600 — faster, more reliable
  • Key specs: Current/voltage rating, firmware compatibility, ESC protocol

Flight Controller – The Brain of Drone

  • Central hub connecting ESCs, GPS, Telemetry, RC Input, sensors
  • Must have: Gyroscope + Accelerometer (IMU) for auto-balance
  • Controlled by firmware (open source: ArduPilot, Betaflight; proprietary: DJI)
  • Popular boards: Pixhawk, Naze32, Revolution Revo

Sensors

  • Accelerometer: Measures acceleration on X, Y, Z axes; detects tilt. Static horizontal: X=0g, Y=0g, Z=1g
  • Gyroscope: Measures rotational speed/orientation
  • IMU (Inertial Measurement Unit): Combines gyro + accelerometer
  • Barometer: Altitude measurement via air pressure
  • GPS: Position and navigation

Thrust & Propeller Selection

  • Thrust-to-Weight (T/W) ratio of 2 recommended for stable flight
  • Propeller notation: e.g. 1045 = 10-inch diameter, 4.5-inch pitch
  • Total thrust = thrust per motor × number of motors
💡 Exam Tip: T/W ratio of 2 means drone can carry payload equal to its own weight while maintaining control.

Software Used in Drones

Assembling, Testing & Quality Check ⭐

HIGH Priority ▾

Unit 6.1 – Drone Assembly

  • Assembly from BOM (Bill of Materials) using supplied drawings
  • Configuration and settings (flight controller calibration)
  • Battery charging procedures
  • Must practice with at least 2 types of drones

Unit 6.2 – Quality Check (QC)

  • Pre-dispatch QC: testing without payload AND with rated payload
  • Check: motor direction, propeller balance, ESC calibration
  • Verify all electrical connections, firmware flashed correctly
  • Deploy drone only after passing QC
💡 Exam Tip: Know the sequence: Assemble → Configure → Charge batteries → Test without payload → Test with payload → Deploy

Types of Drones & Their Applications

Medium Priority ▾

Unit 7.2 – Fixed Wing Drones

  • Need forward motion to generate lift (cannot hover)
  • Long endurance, large coverage area, ideal for mapping/surveying
  • Small fixed-wing: ~50 min endurance, ~80 miles range (e.g. Strix Nano Goblin)

Unit 7.3 – Applications by Type

  • Agriculture: Crop monitoring, pesticide spraying
  • Delivery: Packages in remote areas (e.g. Amazon, DHL)
  • Forest Fire: Infrared + video cameras for smoke detection
  • Surveillance: Airports, stadiums, public events
  • Search & Rescue, Medical delivery, LIDAR scanning

Unit 7.6 – Multi-Rotor Configurations

  • Trirotor (3), Quadcopter (4), Hexacopter (6), Octocopter (8)
  • Shapes: T-shape, Y-shape, Plus (+), X-shape
  • Single-Rotor: Helicopter-like, LIDAR, heavy payload; complex & expensive

Unit 7.7 – Fixed Wing vs Multi-Rotor Comparison

  • Fixed Wing: Longer flight time, greater range, needs runway/launch area
  • Multi-Rotor: VTOL capability (hover), better maneuverability, shorter flight time
  • Hybrid VTOL: Combines advantages of both

Principles of Flying & Drone Simulator ⭐

HIGH Priority ▾

Unit 8.1 – Basic Principles of Flight

  • Bernoulli's Principle: Faster airflow = lower pressure; creates lift over aerofoil
  • Lift is created by pressure difference across rotating blades
  • 4 Forces: Lift (up), Weight (down), Thrust (forward), Drag (backward)

Unit 8.2 – Applying to Drones

  • Propellers act as rotating aerofoils
  • Aerodynamics is same for fixed-wing and propeller-based aircraft
  • CFD (Computational Fluid Dynamics) used for aerodynamic design optimization

Unit 8.4 – Flight Operations

  • Pre-flight checks, ground operations, taxi procedures
  • Takeoff & Landing: Affected by wind direction, runway alignment
  • Flight maneuvers follow procedures to demonstrate aircraft performance
  • Key formula: Pitch-Power-Configuration-Trim
  • Performance calculations are optimistic — real-world performance may vary
💡 Exam Tip: CFD parameters: payload, number/speed of propellers, propeller aerodynamics, spacing between propellers, lift coefficient, thrust generated.

🔑 Key Terms & Definitions

UAV
Unmanned Aerial Vehicle — any drone/aircraft operated without a pilot on board
BLDC Motor
Brushless DC Motor — uses electronic commutation, high efficiency, long life, used in most drones
ESC
Electronic Speed Controller — converts DC power to 3-phase AC to control BLDC motor speed
IMU
Inertial Measurement Unit — combines gyroscope + accelerometer to measure orientation and motion
KV Rating
RPM per volt of input (e.g. 935KV motor at 2V = 1870 RPM). NOT kilovolts.
Flight Controller
The central processing brain of the drone; manages all inputs and controls motors
Throttle
Up/down movement — controlled by varying all motor speeds equally
Pitch
Forward/backward movement about the lateral (side-to-side) axis
Roll
Side-to-side tilting about the longitudinal (front-to-back) axis
Yaw
Rotation about the vertical axis — turning left or right
Bernoulli's Principle
Faster-moving air has lower pressure; the pressure difference creates lift over an aerofoil
CFD
Computational Fluid Dynamics — software simulation to predict airflow and optimize aerodynamic design
Thrust-to-Weight (T/W)
Ratio of total thrust to drone weight. Minimum 2:1 recommended for stable, controllable flight
BOM
Bill of Materials — complete list of all components needed to build a drone
VTOL
Vertical Take-Off and Landing — ability to take off/land without a runway (multi-rotors, hybrid VTOL)
Pixhawk
Popular open-source flight controller board supporting GPS, telemetry, multiple sensors
DShot
Digital ESC protocol (DShot150/300/600) — higher number = faster communication, better response
Gyroscope
Sensor measuring rotational velocity/angular rate about each axis
Accelerometer
Measures linear acceleration and tilt angle; static hover: X=0g, Y=0g, Z=1g
LIDAR
Light Detection and Ranging — sensor using laser pulses for 3D mapping; used in single-rotor drones
